      LAY OF THE LAND

      What each thermal area does best

      Yellowstone’s basins are not interchangeable. Each has different chemistry, plumbing and scenery.

      01

      Geyser

      Constricted plumbing traps pressure until hot water erupts.

      02

      Hot spring

      Open circulation releases heat continuously, often supporting vivid microbes.

      03

      Mudpot

      Acid, limited water and clay create bubbling, splattering mud.

      04

      Fumarole

      A steam vent—the hottest type, with too little water to form a pool.

      01

      ERUPTIONS

      Upper Geyser Basin

      The world’s densest concentration of predictable geysers. This is the place to watch water erupt rather than simply admire color.

      • Old Faithful
      • Grand, Castle and Riverside geysers
      • Morning Glory Pool
      • Black Sand Basin nearby
      Allow 2–4 hours
      02

      COLOR + SCALE

      Midway Geyser Basin

      Compact but monumental: Grand Prismatic and the steaming blue expanse of Excelsior Geyser Crater.

      • Boardwalk for Excelsior and close views
      • Separate Fairy Falls trailhead for the overlook
      Allow 1½–2 hours for both
      03

      VARIETY + SUNSET

      Lower Geyser Basin

      Yellowstone’s broadest mix of geysers, springs, mudpots and fumaroles, spread along the Firehole River.

      • Fountain Paint Pot and Clepsydra
      • Great Fountain and White Dome
      • Firehole Lake Drive
      Allow 1½–3 hours
      04

      EXTREME GEOLOGY

      Norris Geyser Basin

      The park’s hottest, most acidic and most changeable thermal area. Porcelain is exposed; Back Basin is forested.

      • Steamboat—the tallest active geyser
      • Porcelain Basin overlook
      • Emerald Spring and Echinus
      Allow 1½–2½ hours
      05

      LAKE + MORNING LIGHT

      West Thumb

      Thermal pools directly on Yellowstone Lake, combining mountain, water and geothermal scenery in one compact boardwalk.

      • Abyss and Black pools
      • Fishing Cone
      • Lakeshore boardwalk
      Allow 60–90 minutes
      06

      TRAVERTINE

      Mammoth Hot Springs

      Not a geyser basin. Hot water moving through limestone builds white and orange travertine terraces.

      • Palette and Canary springs
      • Minerva Terrace
      • Upper Terrace Drive
      Allow 1½–2½ hours

      Artists Paintpots

      A compact one-mile loop with colorful springs, steam vents and especially active mudpots on the upper loop.

      Mud Volcano

      Acidic, noisy and sulfurous. Dragon’s Mouth and Churning Caldron feel more violent than picturesque.

      Black Sand Basin

      A small Upper Basin satellite with Emerald Pool, Sunset Lake and Cliff Geyser—excellent near evening.

      OPTIONAL EXPERIENCES

      Choose one—not all three

      Adding a major activity means dropping a basin or shortening the canyon. The core itinerary is already full.

      BEST ROUTE FIT

      Roosevelt horseback ride

      1–2 hours · Sunday

      Fits between Tower and Mammoth. Scheduled operating season runs through September 7; reserve immediately.

      MORE ACTIVE

      Yellowstone River rafting

      About 3 hours · Sunday

      Launches from Gardiner. Replace horseback riding and shorten Tower or Mammoth; return into the park for Swan Lake sunset.

      CALMER ALTERNATIVE

      Yellowstone Lake boat tour

      About 1 hour · Saturday

      Operating season extends through September 20. It would replace part of Norris or the longer canyon walks.

      PRACTICAL NOTES

      Timing, safety and reality checks

      Drive-time assumptions

      • Friday finish: approximately 9:00–9:15 PM
      • Saturday finish: approximately 9:00–9:20 PM
      • Saturday: target 7:15 AM after breakfast and lunch pickup; 7:30 AM is workable
      • Sunday: target 7:00 AM for wildlife; 7:30 AM is workable
      • Gardiner to BZN: about 1½ hours; airport around 10:00–10:20 PM

      Construction buffers

      • Up to 15 minutes near Firehole River Bridge
      • Up to 15 minutes around Bridge Bay
      • Up to 30 minutes east of Tower Junction
      • Wildlife jams can add 30–60+ minutes anywhere

      Wildlife and hiking

      • Carry accessible bear spray and know how to use it
      • Stay 100 yards from bears and wolves
      • Stay 25 yards from bison, elk and other wildlife
      • Avoid solo backcountry hiking around dawn and dusk
